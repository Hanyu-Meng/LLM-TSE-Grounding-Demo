Speaker-Consistent Grounding for Confusion-Resilient LLM-Based Target Speech Extraction

Repair the target-speaker evidence first; then constrain the optional LLM speech generator.

Audio examples Grounding limits Metrics Code

Status: Frozen evaluations and listening examples are complete.

Overview

Two failures are easy to conflate. A deterministic waveform TSE system does not introduce autoregressive token hallucination, but under noise it may sound distorted or extract the wrong speaker. An autoregressive speech model can raise estimated perceptual quality, yet its learned prior can change words or speaker identity. Our rule is therefore simple: repair the target evidence first, then ground generation on that repaired evidence.

Key result. On the frozen 6,000-trial natural-noise TEST set, CDCS-5 direct reduces WER from 46.17% to 35.64%, content-switch rate from 15.57% to 7.95%, and acoustic-switch rate from 14.57% to 7.17% relative to the primary extractor.

Design rule Repair target identity first; ground autoregressive generation second.

Method

Conditioning-diverse candidate selection (CDCS-5) selects the most enrollment-consistent waveform from five frozen TSE candidates. It can be returned directly as the content-fidelity endpoint, or tokenized as evidence for Qwen-TSE. The Qwen-TSE speech prior supplies the estimated-quality gain; CSG keeps its next-token choices close to the repaired evidence. This ordering matters: CSG limits generation drift, but cannot repair a wrong-speaker anchor by itself.

Token-level grounding intuition

The speech tokenizer represents each acoustic token with eight ternary FSQ coordinates. Grounding changes token selection inside Qwen-TSE; it does not change the CDCS-5 selector.

Frozen Natural Noisy TEST · 6,000 trials · post-hoc listening audit

Why can LLM-based TSE have high WER?

These six cases expose lexical drift, decoding failures, speaker/content confusion, and genuine grounding successes or regressions under one ASR configuration. Play the complete waveforms first, then reveal the transcripts. The cases are illustrations, not prevalence estimates, and were never used to tune a system or replace the frozen TEST metrics.

ASR used throughout. Every transcript and WER chip in this listening audit uses Whisper large-v3 with frozen greedy English decoding, repetition penalty 1.10, and batch size 32. The target reference is transcribed with the same configuration. No human reference transcription was added, so the listening interpretation remains deliberately cautious.

Evaluation metrics

Introduced in this work

Two operational diagnostics for speaker confusion

WER alone cannot tell whether an output followed the competing speaker. We therefore define paired, reference-based indicators for lexical identity and acoustic identity.

C-sw.↓ lower is better

Content-switch rate

A trial is flagged when the fixed canonical ASR transcript of the output has lower WER to the clean interferer transcript than to the clean target transcript. Ties are not switches.

csw,n = 1[WER(τI,n, τ̂n) < WER(τT,n, τ̂n)]
A-sw.↓ lower is better

Acoustic-switch rate

A frozen CosyVoice CAMPPlus evaluator flags a trial when the output is more cosine-similar to the clean interferer than to the clean target. Ties are not switches.

mspk,n = cos(f(sT,n), f(ŝn)) − cos(f(sI,n), f(ŝn)) asw,n = 1[mspk,n < 0]
C-sw. = 100%/N Σ csw,n · A-sw. = 100%/N Σ asw,n

These are study-defined operational diagnostics, not standardized perceptual metrics or proof of semantic or speaker correctness. Clean target and interferer references are used for evaluation only; CDCS deployment uses neither. The CAMPPlus evaluator is independent of the frozen ECAPA selector.

WER ↓
WER = (S + D + I) / Nref × 100%. Reported as the uncapped utterance mean against a same-ASR clean-target transcript.
Spk. margin ↑
Target-minus-interferer CAMPPlus cosine similarity; a negative value triggers A-sw.
DNSMOS ↑
Predicted P.808 quality for clean speech and P.835 OVRL for noisy speech.
UTMOS ↑
Reference-free predicted naturalness; it does not establish content or speaker identity.
